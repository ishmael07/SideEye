Three ways it has your back

SideEye watches for faces, not content. It never looks at what's on your screen.

You look away

The blur sweeps in from the side you turned away from, so the part you can still see stays readable until you've fully turned.

You step away

Coffee run, doorbell, meeting. No face in frame means the whole screen hides in under half a second, and clears when you're back.

Someone side-eyes you

A second face appears behind you and the screen is gone before they can read a word. Head-tracking earbuds can't see that. A camera can.

A camera app you can actually trust

A privacy tool that phones home would be a joke. This one has no network code at all, and you can check.

  • Everything stays on your MacFace tracking runs on Apple's on-device Vision framework. Frames live in memory for a few milliseconds and are never saved.
  • No network, no account, no analyticsThe app never opens a connection. It's open source, so that's a claim you can verify instead of trust.
  • Camera off when it should bePause it, lock your Mac, or close the lid and the camera turns off. The green light is always honest.
  • It never sees your screenThe blur is done by macOS itself, live. SideEye doesn't need Screen Recording permission and never asks for it.
